## Lessons
- **What failed:** The negative CMF was the dominant signal. Unlike XOM (+0.04) and NVDA (+0.038) where accumulation during the selloff confirmed a pullback-in-uptrend, TJX's CMF (-0.179 at exit) showed distribution — institutional selling, not stealth buying. The oversold extreme (RSI ~26) persisted for days without producing a bounce. Oversold alone is NOT a reliable trigger when volume confirms distribution.
- **Framework gap (critical):** For "oversold-in-uptrend" trades, the structural invalidation (SMA200 break) should drive the stop, not just the volatility bands. The $149.00 stop was placed $3.38 below SMA200 ($152.68) — too far. The real invalidation level was SMA200, and price sat only $0.17 above it at the last review. A stop just below SMA200 would have exited earlier at a smaller loss and freed capital.
- **Signal to UNDERWEIGHT:** RSI < 30 with negative CMF = a "trap" oversold, not a bounce setup. The XOM/NVDA framework requires positive CMF as a hard gate. TJX should have been rejected at entry for failing that gate, same as CVX (CMF -0.10) and CAT (CMF -0.022).
- **Net:** The negative-CMF variant of the framework is 0-for-1. The clean variant (positive CMF) is 2-for-2 (XOM +2.97%, NVDA +3.97%). Going forward, CMF sign is a hard entry filter, not a "differentiator" we can override with fundamentals.
